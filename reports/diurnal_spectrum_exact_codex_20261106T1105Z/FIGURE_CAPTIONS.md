**Diurnal spectrum of the measured clock comparison.** a, The 9.5–12.5 µHz band, including Q₁ and O₁; the shaded region is enlarged in b. b, The P₁, exact-24-h and K₁ neighbourhood. Blue shows the original 3,299 R7 observation bins at their recorded UTC times, with equal weights and 54 floating segment constants. Orange is the spectrum after subtracting the fixed unit-amplitude R7 tidal prediction in the time domain. Neither the amplitude nor the phase of that prediction is re-estimated for subtraction; no gaps are filled. The spectrum and potential conversion use the same nominal 600-s one-sided least-squares convention as the semidiurnal display. The left axis is in (m²/s²)²/µHz and the right in 10⁻³⁵/µHz, related by S_W = c⁴ S_y. Dashed lines identify nominal astronomical frequencies and exact 24 h, not newly detected constituents. The two curves share observations. Residual power can contain measurement noise, model error and unmodelled signals; increased or decreased residual power is not an independent detection test.

**Effect of subtracting the strong semidiurnal prediction.** The same measured record and spectral operator are used for all curves. Blue shows the observed spectrum, dashed purple the residual after subtracting only the fixed M₂+N₂+S₂+K₂ prediction, and orange the residual after subtracting the fixed full R7 prediction. All subtractions occur in the time domain at observed timestamps, without adjusting tidal amplitudes or filling gaps. The K₁-frequency scan power decreases by approximately 64% after semidiurnal-only subtraction and 69% after full-template subtraction. This sensitivity demonstrates that interpreting the diurnal scan requires accounting for leakage from the strong semidiurnal band. The percentages are changes in scan power with coherent cross terms, not additive estimates of constituent power fractions.
