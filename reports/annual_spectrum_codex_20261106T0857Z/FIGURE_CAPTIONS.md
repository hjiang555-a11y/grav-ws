# Candidate main-text panel

**Observed spectral peaks near the lunar and solar semidiurnal frequencies.** **a**, Generalized sinusoidal spectrum of all 3,299 accepted ten-minute means spanning 353.39 days. The shaded range is expanded in **b**. **b**, The same spectrum around the semidiurnal band; dashed lines mark the nominal astronomical M₂ and S₂ frequencies, and dots mark the nearby observed local maxima. The observations are retained at their original UTC timestamps. Missing intervals are neither filled nor removed from the calendar. At every trial frequency, sine and cosine terms are estimated jointly with 54 segment constants, with equal weights. No fitted tidal prediction or empirical daily waveform is subtracted. The ordinate is the one-sided Lomb–Scargle density under the nominal 600-s cadence convention, 600 s times the reduction in residual sum of squares. This equals the ordinary one-sided density for complete uniform sampling; for this gapped record, its integral is not a calibrated continuous-year variance. The spectrum uses the same observations as the tidal estimates and provides no additional independent detection probability. Sampling-window aliases are retained and assessed separately. A peak at 12 h identifies a frequency, not by itself its gravitational origin.

# Accompanying diagnostic figure

**Sampling and analysis checks for the observed spectrum.** **a**, Squared Fourier modulus of the observed time mask, normalized to unity at zero frequency offset. The dashed line marks the M₂–S₂ separation. **b**, Responses of the same spectral estimator to the individual unit-scale predicted M₂ and S₂ signals evaluated only at observed timestamps. Each curve is normalized to its own maximum. These are theoretical window-response diagnostics, not additional observations or gap-filled data. **c**, Spectra of the unchanged measured means under segment or period constants and equal or original R7 noise weights. The ordinate is the reduction in weighted residual sum of squares divided by the number of observations; weights are normalized to mean one for each design. All curves share the same 3,299 observations. The constants, weights and normalization are specified for each curve. These checks assess peak-location sensitivity and do not calibrate a detection probability.
