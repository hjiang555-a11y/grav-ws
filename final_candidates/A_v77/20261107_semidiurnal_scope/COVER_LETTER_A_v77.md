Dear Editors,

We submit “Resolving lunar and solar tidal responses with an optical clock baseline” as a Nature Article. Optical clocks measure changes in gravitational potential through their relative rates. We use the changing lunar–solar phase to separate neighbouring semidiurnal responses in 545.9 hours of Shanghai–Wuhan observations spread over 353 days. Astronomical phase remains defined through interruptions, allowing segments with unknown frequency reference levels to constrain common physical responses. The spectrum has local peaks near the principal frequencies, 0.78 µHz apart; joint M₂+N₂ and S₂+K₂ estimates are 1.00 ± 0.13 and 0.93 ± 0.36. The measurement tests a combined solid-Earth and ocean-loading prediction on a coastal–inland baseline.

The summer observations overlap Hou et al., arXiv:2610.34250, on the absolute Yb/Sr frequency ratio. This manuscript addresses time-dependent potential components across an extended record, without updating that ratio or reusing its significance. The related manuscript and overlap statement will accompany submission.

Stahl et al., arXiv:2610.39635, reports independent, contemporaneous tidal observations with a European clock network and is cited as related research. Nature Portfolio excludes preprints from prior publication and from its assessment of advance.
