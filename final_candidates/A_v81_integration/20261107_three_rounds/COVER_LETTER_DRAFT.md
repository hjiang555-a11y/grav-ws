Dear Editors,

We submit “Tidal potential measured on a coastal–inland optical-clock baseline” for consideration as a Nature Article. A strontium clock in coastal Shanghai and an ytterbium clock in inland Wuhan, 692 km apart, detect tidal changes in their relative gravitational potential. The complete prediction has a measured scale of 0.96 ± 0.11 on 545.9 hours spread across 353 days. A reduction within 54 interrupted observing segments retains the astronomical phase without transferring their frequency levels. We then constrain two neighbouring semidiurnal groups, with their internal amplitudes and phases fixed, while keeping same-frequency sensitivity distinct from the overall detection.

The summer acquisition windows overlap those used in Hou et al., arXiv:2610.34250, whose primary objective is an absolute frequency ratio. The present reduction contains 273.925 summer member-hours and 271.999 additional early member-hours; the attached acquisition table lists their dates. We do not update the ratio or transfer the statistical significance of its tidal-correlation check. The experimental team must confirm the exact cross-manuscript member-mask overlap before submission.

We cite independent, contemporaneous European results, Stahl et al., arXiv:2610.39635.
