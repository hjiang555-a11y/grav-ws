Dear Editors,

We submit for consideration “Optical clocks distinguish neighbouring semidiurnal tides”. A comparison between coastal Shanghai and inland Wuhan accumulated 546 hours of valid observations over 353 days. The changing lunar–solar phase allows two nearby semidiurnal responses to be estimated with independent frequency offsets for each observing segment. The measured spectrum has local peaks near the principal lunar and solar frequencies; the joint physical model gives lunar and neighbouring-group responses of 1.00 ± 0.13 and 0.93 ± 0.36. Ocean loading contributes about one third of the combined prediction, although its coefficient is not separately resolved.

Some summer observations overlap Hou et al., arXiv:2610.34250, on the absolute Yb/Sr frequency ratio. This manuscript addresses the extended record's time-dependent tidal components, does not update the ratio and does not reuse its significance. We will provide the related manuscript and confirmed overlap and submission-status statement.

Stahl et al., arXiv:2610.39635, provides complementary contemporaneous observations on European baselines. Nature Portfolio excludes preprints from prior publication and from its assessment of advance. We make no date-based priority claim. Data and code will be available through the author-approved editorial access route.
