Dear Editors,

We submit “Tidal potential measured on a coastal–inland optical-clock baseline” as a Nature Article. Shanghai strontium and Wuhan ytterbium clocks accumulated 545.9 hours over 353 days, with independent offsets across 54 segments. The changing lunar–solar phase supplies an external reference through interruptions. Peaks near principal frequencies 0.78 µHz apart accompany joint M₂+N₂ and S₂+K₂ responses of 1.00 ± 0.13 and 0.93 ± 0.36. Ocean loading contributes about 30% of the predicted M₂ amplitude. Separating gravitational signals in interrupted networks serves both Earth monitoring and the redshift corrections needed for common timekeeping.

Summer observations overlap Hou et al., arXiv:2610.34250, on the absolute Yb/Sr frequency ratio. Here we address temporal potential components across an extended record, without updating that ratio or reusing its significance. The related manuscript and overlap statement will accompany submission.

Stahl et al., arXiv:2610.39635, reports independent, contemporaneous European network observations. We cite its complementary tracking of composite tidal variations. Nature Portfolio excludes preprints from prior publication and from its assessment of advance. Data and code are prepared for referee access through the authors' confirmed access route.
