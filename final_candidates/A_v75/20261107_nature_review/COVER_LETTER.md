Dear Editors,

We submit “Tidal potential measured on a coastal–inland optical-clock baseline” as a Nature Article. Optical-clock comparisons turn gravitational changes in clock rates into observations of the changing Earth. We measure tidal potential variations between Shanghai and Wuhan and resolve neighbouring semidiurnal responses whose principal frequencies are 0.78 µHz apart. Joint M₂+N₂ and S₂+K₂ coefficients are 1.00 ± 0.13 and 0.93 ± 0.36. The record contains 545.9 hours over 353 days, with independent offsets across 54 segments. Astronomical phase connects these interrupted observations. Ocean loading contributes about 30% of the predicted M₂ amplitude. The measurement demonstrates both tidal sensitivity and high spectral resolution on a continental clock baseline.

Summer observations overlap Hou et al., arXiv:2610.34250, on the absolute Yb/Sr frequency ratio. Here we address temporal potential components across an extended record, without updating that ratio or reusing its significance. The related manuscript and overlap statement will accompany submission.

Stahl et al., arXiv:2610.39635, reports independent, contemporaneous tidal observations with a European clock network and is cited in the manuscript. Nature Portfolio excludes preprints from prior publication and from its assessment of advance. Data and code are prepared for referee access through the authors' confirmed access route.
