Dear Editors,

We submit for consideration “Tidal potential measured on a coastal–inland optical-clock baseline”. A strontium clock in Shanghai and a ytterbium clock in Wuhan, separated by 692 km, compare their rates through a 1,350-km fibre link. Interrupted observations sample the changing lunar–solar phase, allowing simultaneous estimation of the M₂+N₂ and S₂+K₂ semidiurnal responses. The coastal–inland geometry makes ocean loading about one third of the predicted variation. The manuscript connects this physical prediction to the full-record phase display, measured semidiurnal spectrum and simultaneous transmission control.

Some summer observations overlap Hou et al., arXiv:2610.34250, which reports the absolute Yb/Sr frequency ratio. This manuscript instead addresses grouped time-dependent potential responses across the extended record; it does not update the absolute ratio or inherit its tidal significance. We will provide the related manuscript and the authors' confirmed overlap and submission-status statement.

We cite Stahl et al., arXiv:2610.39635, as complementary contemporaneous observations on European baselines.
